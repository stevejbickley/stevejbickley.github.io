# Research figures and metric sources

## Thesis figures

Extracted from Steve J. Bickley's 2023 QUT thesis, *Bridging Complexity and Behavioural Economics: The Constrained Methods Matrix*, DOI [10.5204/thesis.eprints.244769](https://doi.org/10.5204/thesis.eprints.244769).

| Site asset | Thesis figure | Printed page | PDF page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `cmm-matrix` | Fig. 1, extended Constrained Methods Matrix | 13 | 34 |
| `nested-feedback` | Fig. 5, feedback across three hierarchical levels | 56 | 77 |
| `ai-economics-growth` | Fig. 31, growth of AI-related economics papers | 334 | 355 |
| `ai-economics-networks` | Fig. 34, networks of JEL subject codes | 342 | 363 |

The original PDF pages were rendered and the figures cropped from them. Labels, colours, axes and data were retained. The website uses lossless WebP derivatives and full-resolution PNGs. Nothing was redrawn or generated. The original thesis and seminar files are not included in the public site.

The CMM is credited to the thesis and Bickley & Torgler (2023), building on Minsky, Sloman and Singh. Fig. 5's chapter discussion draws on Simon (1962) and Ceddia et al. (2013). The two AI-economics figures are also associated with [Bickley, Chan & Torgler (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11192-022-04294-w) and are historical study results, not current publication counts.
